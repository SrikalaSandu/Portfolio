● Published · ACM SIGACCESS ASSETS 2025

Interactive Form Filling Assistant on Smart Glasses for Blind Users

R. R. Khan, S. Sandu, J. Hong · Interactive Computing Lab, Stevens · Oct 2025

Printed forms are everywhere, from doctor's offices to banks, and blind people usually need someone else to fill them out. That costs privacy and independence. IFFA runs on Vuzix M400 glasses: it finds the fields on a page, tracks the user's fingertip and pen, and guides them by voice. A reading mode says what each field asks; a writing mode guides the pen to the right spot.

my contributions

  • Built the Android app for the glasses in Kotlin with the Camera2 API, with voice feedback at every step
  • Implemented real-time form-field detection in OpenCV 4.9: Canny edges, contour analysis, perspective warp
  • Integrated the vision and language components with an AWS backend
  • Ran the literature review on assistive form filling and smart-glasses accessibility
  • Evaluated forms from 25 blind participants by hand and with an IoU algorithm
  • Analyzed Likert usability ratings and built the charts in R

the story behind the GPA

I walked across the stage at Stevens with a 3.8. But if you asked me what I learned there, I wouldn't start with a class.

I'd start with a notebook. In my first semester, a blind classmate needed someone to take lecture notes, so I did. That small favor turned into a job at the Disability Services office, where I sat beside blind students during their exams, reading each question aloud and writing down every answer exactly as they gave it. By my third semester I was also walking students to their classes across campus. I learned more about patience, trust, and design from those walks than from any lecture.

So when a research assistant role opened up in accessibility, it felt like it was meant for me, and I still almost didn't apply.

"You miss every shot you don't take, so take it."

My dear friend Rifat, who talked me into applying

I took it. Working alongside a blind researcher, I helped build IFFA, an Android app on Vuzix smart glasses that lets blind users fill out printed forms on their own, starting with the medical paperwork most of us never think twice about.

That work became a co-authored paper at ACM ASSETS 2025, one of the top venues in accessibility research. It's the moment I'm proudest of so far, and I don't plan for it to be the last.

Thank you to the professors who shaped how I think, especially Professor Jonggi Hong; to my family; and to every student who trusted me to help them through their day.
